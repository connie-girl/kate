From 2015-2018, I studied Quechua intensively as part of my B.A. in Linguistics. Quechua (endonym: Runa Simi) is a Pre-Columbian language spoken by millions in the Andes. It was the primary language of the Inca Empire.

Quechua has a neat phonological system: three vowels and a voiceless, aspirated, and ejective series of stops. Runa Simi is agglutinating, and word order is basically free. Derivational morphology is very rich.

FLAS (Foreign Language Area Studies) is a national grant program that funds the study of critical languages. I was a FLAS fellow for the 2017-2018 academic year. I was awarded the fellowship again for the summer of 2018, for which I traveled to Cusco, Peru for two months of intensive study.

As part of my studies, I visited rural communities outside of Cusco to study the cultural practices of the region and practice language skills. We also distributed educational materials to the communities, like Quechua storybooks. Quechua remains largely an oral language.
